“The Watchmaker’s Mistake”

In a quiet village, an old watchmaker named Elias spent his nights tinkering with gears and springs. One stormy evening, he built something unusual — a pocket watch with no ticking sound and a faint blue glow inside.

When he pressed its crown, the rain outside froze midair. The lightning stayed in the sky, unmoving. Elias stepped outside and found himself in the same village, but younger — the people he knew were children again, and his shop was freshly painted.

He realized he had built a time machine.

At first, he used it to relive his happiest memories — his wife’s laughter, his son’s first steps. But every time he went back, he noticed small things were different: a streetlamp missing, a cat that never appeared again.

Eventually, he tried to return to his own time — but the watch no longer worked. The hands spun endlessly. He wandered the village, seeing versions of it from years and decades apart, but never the one he called home.

Some say the old watchmaker still walks through time, searching for the moment he first pressed the crown — the one mistake he can never unmake.
